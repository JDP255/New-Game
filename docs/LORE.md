# VALKIMSMOR — The Lore of Kim and Zerkskis

> *"Eight feathers to bear the body, and a ninth for the one who falls beside you."*
> — The Rule of the Wingbearers

This is the canon of *Valkimsmor: Wings of Kim*. It's an original mythology. Its themes come from the Bible: creation by a spoken Word, a fall through pride, the lost brother, sacrifice, mercy over vengeance, and redemption. None of it retells a Bible story or quotes scripture. The world has its own holy book, **The Canticle**.

---

## I. The Ages of the World

### The Age of the Anthem (Creation)
Before anything existed there was the **First Voice**. It does not shout commands. It *sings*. Its first note was **light**, its second was **longing**, and every living thing is woven from the two. That song, **the Anthem**, is still playing under every sound in the world. Wingbearers say that in perfect silence, high above the clouds, you can still hear it.

The Voice is never shown or named beyond that. It answers through gifts, never on demand. That's the heart of the whole story.

### The Age of the Lifting
The Voice lifted the land of **Kim** on its breath and set it above the clouds. It wasn't a reward. It was *a promise kept in plain sight*: anyone below could look up and see that promises are kept.

- Those who went up became the **Kimari**.
- Those who chose to stay and tend the deep roots of the world became the **Rootfolk**.

For a thousand years the two peoples traded, married and sang together. There was no word for "Zerkskis" yet.

The isles of Kim are held up by golden updrafts called the **Rivers of Breath**. Kim's sun is the **Last Lantern**, the final unbroken piece of the first light, hung in the sky by the Voice.

### The Age of Wings
From the **Ember of Dawn** (a fragment of the first light kept in the Cathedral of the Anthem), the Voice gave living wings to guardians called the **Wingbearers**. At their height they numbered **three hundred and twelve**.

A Wingbearer:
- swears the **Six Oathmarks** over a lifetime, each etched into their armour in living gold;
- keeps the **Rule of the Ninth Feather**: every wing holds one feather never used for flight. When a brother falls, you pluck it and lay it in his hand;
- carries a blade forged in Kim. Their swords were never meant to kill, only to set free.

### The Fall (the Age of Smoke)
The first and brightest Wingbearer was **Zekeriah**. He heard the Anthem more clearly than anyone alive, and it wasn't enough. He wanted the Voice to *speak to him*, plainly, on his terms, now.

One night he climbed to the Ember of Dawn to take it. The Ember didn't resist him. **It simply burned.** His wings became smoke, and he fell through the clouds into the Rootlands.

His student, a young Wingbearer named **Valkimsmor**, was standing beside him that night. He saw what his teacher meant to do, and he didn't stop him. He has carried that for three hundred years.

### The Hollowing
Among the Rootfolk, the fallen Zekeriah offered a gift: **an end to fear.** It cost only a name.

Those who accepted were filled with a quiet darkness that made them brave, obedient and tireless. Slowly it erased who they had been. This is **the Hollowing**. The Rootfolk became the **Zerkskis**, "the people of the dark", and forgot the name was ever an insult.

The Zerkskis are **not evil by nature**. They are bound. When a Wingbearer strikes a Hollowed soul down, the darkness breaks. What rises isn't a corpse but **a soul of light**, carrying its name home. (In gameplay, those lights fly into Valkimsmor and heal him: *mercy sustains the merciful.*)

### The Dimming (the present day)
Zekeriah has spent three hundred years building the **Spire of Ascension**, a stair to the Last Lantern. When it's finished he means to swallow the light of the world so he never has to ask for anything again. Every year the Spire grows, the Lantern dims, and the golden trees of Kim drop their leaves for the first time in history.

Of 312 Wingbearers, one remains.

## II. Characters

### Valkimsmor, the Last Wingbearer
In the old tongue: **Val** (oath), **Kim** (the lifted land), **Smor** (unbroken). *"The Oath of Kim, Unbroken."*

An orphan of the Rootlands. As a child he was carried up to Kim by the Wingbearer who would become his teacher: Zekeriah. So Valkimsmor is **Rootfolk by birth**, of the same blood as the people he fights. He speaks rarely and has never broken a vow. He believes that makes up for the one night he stood still. It doesn't, and the story is about him learning what does.

He has laid down **three hundred and eleven** ninth-feathers in the hands of his fallen brothers. His ninth feather is the last one left.

- **Veritas**, his sword, was forged by Molochar the Smith. Its core glows with a runic light.
- His **chest sigil** is the Cross-and-Halo of Kim, the mark of the Anthem.
- His **halo of Glory** appears when his spirit is full. That's when he can call down **Judgment**, pillars of light from the Lantern itself.

### Zekeriah, the First Wingbearer, Lord of Zerkskis
Brilliant, wounded, magnetic. He isn't a cackling villain. He's a teacher who couldn't bear to *trust*. He believes the Voice abandoned him because it only ever sang and never answered. His great sin is refusing to receive the light as a gift, which is pride dressed as a hunger for truth.

He wears a crown of black thorns of his own making. His wings are smoke. **He has kept one white feather from before the fall, hidden at the top of his Spire.** He could never bring himself to burn it.

His offer to everyone is the same: *what you lost, in exchange for the reason you were worth giving it to.*

### Elder Ithiel
Keeper of the Cathedral of the Anthem and the oldest living soul in Kim. She remembers Zekeriah as a boy who asked too many questions. She still prays for him **by name** every morning, which no one else in Kim will do. She's the moral centre of the story: *"When your blade strikes them down, it does not end them. It sets them free."*

### Captain Dorian of the Isles
Commander of Kim's last army: farmers, bell-ringers and fletchers with borrowed spears. Brave, bitter, and ashamed that for forty days he couldn't make himself walk down into the valley to face Gorlath.

### Gorlath, the Many-in-One
Not one giant but **a hundred Hollowed soldiers** who each wished never to feel small again. The Hollowing welded them into a single colossus. Each footstep is a hundred men walking in step, and each roar is a hundred men screaming in one voice. They still feel small, all hundred of them. When he falls, he breaks into a hundred lights, each one carrying a name.

### Molochar, the Smith Who Became Fire
The greatest smith Kim ever knew, and the one who forged Veritas. He asked the Voice for a fire that would never go out, then grew tired of waiting and took the question to Zekeriah. Now he *is* the fire, a furnace in the shape of a man, forging the weapons of the Hollowed day and night. He once taught Valkimsmor that *"a smith fears impurity, never heat."* When he's freed, his last words are to ask whether the sword he made is still true.

### The Stranger in the Flame
A figure of pure light who walks beside those who enter fire *for someone else*. He never speaks. He's never seen twice by the same person. The Canticle doesn't name him, and the elders won't guess. (He's the story's quietest and most deliberate echo.)

### Tehomar, the Deep That Hungers
A vast serpent born in the Obsidian Sea, which is made of **every tear the Hollowed ever wept alone**. Tehomar isn't truly evil. It's what loneliness becomes when it's left long enough in the dark. It feeds on grief that no one comforts.

### Mira the Lampwoman
A citizen of Kor-Jerah who kept a lamp burning in her window through every year of the forgetting, though she couldn't remember whom she was waiting for. She sheltered two scouts of Kim when no one else would. When the walls fell, her house, built into the wall, stood untouched. *The stones remembered her name even when she did not.*

## III. Places

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Aerie of Kim** | A thousand floating isles above a golden sea of clouds, joined by the Rivers of Breath. Golden trees, marble ruins, and the **Cathedral of the Anthem** with its rose window of living stained glass. |
| **Ashmourn, the Vale Without Song** | A shepherds' valley where the **Lanternkeepers** kept five hearths so no traveller ever walked more than an hour in the dark. Now drowned in the **Fog of Unmaking**, which whispers *you are alone*. |
| **The Fields of Elah-Kim** | A sunset battlefield between two ridges: the army of Kim to the south, the Hollowed legions to the north, and a brook between them. |
| **Kor-Jerah, City of Stone Oaths** | The greatest city of the Rootfolk, famous for its bell-foundries. Its people swore the **Stone Oath** to forget their past in exchange for walls that could never fall. The walls are made of ten thousand lost names. Outside hang the **Seven Bells of Remembrance**, cast from the first silver of Kim, each tuned to a note of the Anthem. |
| **The Forge of Ash** | Molochar's volcanic furnace-works, where lava rivers run and every blade of the Hollowed is made. |
| **The Obsidian Sea of Tehom** | A black sea of uncomforted grief under a permanent storm. Its tides follow despair rather than the moon. |
| **The Spire of Ascension** | Zekeriah's stair to the Last Lantern, built from the walls of every home in Zerkskis. Each stone was given by someone who was promised the dark would end. |

## IV. The Six Oathmarks

Vows etched into a Wingbearer's armour in living gold. Each one is earned and grants a power.

1. **Truth** *(belt)*: "I will not lie, not even to spare myself." Glory builds faster.
2. **The Steadfast Heart** *(breastplate)*: "I will stand where others ran." More Vigor.
3. **The Swift Tiding** *(greaves)*: "Where hope is needed, I will already be on my way." A second air-dash.
4. **Shelter** *(gauntlet)*: "What is thrown at the weak, I will throw back." Parries reflect bolts.
5. **Rising** *(brow)*: "I will fall. I will not stay fallen." Revive once per trial.
6. **The Voice** *(blade)*: "I will speak only what was first spoken to me." Judgment strikes twice as hard.

## V. The Story: Seven Chapters

1. **The Unbroken Wing** (The Aerie of Kim). The Zerkskis raid Kim. Valkimsmor defends the Cathedral, confesses to Elder Ithiel that he stood still the night Zekeriah fell, and learns that his enemies are his own kin. *Oathmark of Truth.*
2. **The Vale Without Song** (Ashmourn). He rekindles the five Lanternkeeper hearths, pushing back the Fog of Unmaking, and silences **the Whisperer in the Fog**. Dawn returns to the vale for the first time in forty nights. *Oathmark of the Steadfast Heart.*
3. **The Many-in-One** (Elah-Kim). While Kim's army hesitates, one knight walks down to face a colossus, not to kill a giant but to give a hundred men back their names. *"Then why are all hundred of you shaking?"* *Oathmark of the Swift Tiding.*
4. **The Silent Walls** (Kor-Jerah). Instead of breaching the city, he rings the Seven Bells of Remembrance. Ten thousand people remember their names at once, and walls built of forgetting collapse. Only Mira's house stands. *Oathmark of Shelter.*
5. **The Forge of Ash** (Molochar's Furnace). He faces his sword's maker. When the furnace rages sevenfold and the lava rises, **the Stranger in the Flame** walks beside him. Freed, Molochar asks one question: *is the blade still true?* *Oathmark of Rising.*
6. **The Drowned Sky** (Tehom). Crossing the sea of grief, he's tempted: Zekeriah offers to **restore every fallen Wingbearer** if he kneels once. He refuses: *"What you offer is what I lost. What you ask for is the reason it was worth losing."* Then he defeats Tehomar, and one tear in the deep is finally wiped away. *Oathmark of the Voice.*
7. **The Last Lantern** (The Spire of Ascension). He climbs the Spire and fights his teacher through three phases: the Wizard, the Shadows, and the Unbound. Beaten, Zekeriah says *"Finish it. It is what I would do."*

### The Ending
Valkimsmor raises Veritas, then lowers it. *"I know. That is why I will not."*

He kneels, plucks **his own ninth feather**, the last one he has, and lays it in his teacher's hand. It's the Rule of the Ninth Feather: *for the one who falls beside you.*

> **Zekeriah:** "I fell three hundred years ago, Val. You were not beside me."
> **Valkimsmor:** "I am now."

The darkness drains out of Zekeriah. The eclipse breaks. The Last Lantern blazes back, and across Zerkskis ten thousand lights rise from the dark, each carrying a name. Zekeriah weeps and draws out the white feather he kept hidden for three hundred years.

**The last leaf of the Canticle had always been left blank. That morning, it was written.**

## VI. Themes

- **Light is a gift, not a prize.** Zekeriah's fall is refusing to *receive*. Valkimsmor's victory is *giving*.
- **Your enemies are your lost family.** Every foe is Rootfolk, and every kill is a liberation.
- **Mercy over vengeance.** The final act is an offered feather, not a killing blow.
- **Guilt to grace.** Valkimsmor's arc runs from *"I stood still"* to *"I am beside you now."*
- **Small things matter.** A single feather, a lamp in a window, one knight against a hundred.
- **Remembering sets people free.** The Hollowing takes names. The story is about giving them back.
